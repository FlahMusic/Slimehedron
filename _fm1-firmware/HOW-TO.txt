SLIMEHEDRON FOR THE M-VAVE FM-1 (test build)

This folder: the firmware (slimehedron-fm1.fwsc, always the newest build), INSTALL-ON-FM1.bat, VERSIONS.txt (which
build is which). Built on SLOOP 2.8.5, the first SLOOP that saves and updates on the newer FM-1s (UC25HQ80 flash, sold
from late 2026). Tested in fm1-emu (strict mode, the newer flash). NOT yet run on a real FM-1.

BEFORE YOU INSTALL
- Use a data USB cable (not a charge-only one), plugged straight into the pc (no hub). Chrome or Edge.
- Close other apps that use MIDI. Keep the FM-1 charged; don't let the pc sleep; don't unplug while it writes.
- The official firmware's own sounds cannot be backed up by anyone. Anything made on it stays on it until you go back.

INSTALL
1. Double-click INSTALL-ON-FM1.bat (in this folder). A black window opens and stays open: leave it.
2. Your browser opens http://localhost:8766/webapp/installer/ (if not, open it in Chrome or Edge yourself).
3. The page says SLOOP: that is SLOOP's installer, which is what installs this file. Follow it; let it back up.
4. When it is done, the FM-1 restarts: the slime drops in with his jingle, then the tank. Close the black window.
(A firmware file can't be installed from a plain web page you open from disk: Web MIDI needs a local server, which
 is what the .bat starts. No public installer page takes an arbitrary .fwsc file; each firmware ships its own page.)

IF IT DOES NOT START
- Hold OCT- (alone) while switching the FM-1 on: SLOOP's USB rescue. Then install again from the page.
- Two failed boots in a row also drop into the rescue by themselves.
- Last resort: FM-1-transporter (github.com/kurogedelic/FM-1-transporter), or The Firmware Hub's rescue tool
  (it works on the newer flash; take a full backup first).

BACK TO THE OFFICIAL FIRMWARE
- M-VAVE's updater M-UPGRADE (m-vave.com: Downloads > PC Software > M-UPGRADE) with the official FM-1 firmware V16.
- If M-UPGRADE does not find the FM-1: Clipper's installer page (isod89.github.io/clipper-fm1) > Return to the official
  firmware.

PLAYING IT
At power-on the slime guy drops in with a little jingle (any button skips it), then the tank waits: nothing plays
until PLAY (it blinks; the screen says "press play"). Set it up first if you like; a new shape shows at once while
stopped, and the keys play their notes.

It works like a groovebox: ONE BUTTON, ONE PAGE, and every setting lives in exactly one place. On every page the
encoders mean the same kind of thing: SELECT picks what the page works on (a part, a chord, a lane), the four knobs
are its values, ALGORITHM and PRESETS are the page's two extras. Every page but the tank shows its knobs' names and
values on a panel under the (smaller) tank. A page's button again: back to the tank.

PAGE      BUTTON  KNOB 1     KNOB 2    KNOB 3      KNOB 4       SELECT          ALGORITHM          PRESETS
tank      HOME    walls      spin      portals     note length  notes a bar     new shape every    -
song      SCL     tempo      swing     key         scale        rhythm          a chord every      key moves / pinned
mixer     GLO     melody     bass      chords      drums        the strip       its delay send     its reverb send
fx        FX      filter     drive     effect      amount       part / returns  pan                fx preset
sound     ENV     attack     decay     sustain     release      all / a part    glide              the sound
chords    EDIT    root       type      bars        how many     which chord     all up / down      famous ones
drums     SEQ     fill       shift     feel        mine/rhythm  lane            -                  from rhythm / clear

  tank: HOME on the tank makes a new shape. Notes a bar: the melody's density (4, 6, 8, 12, 16; the site aims at 8).
        New shape every 32, 64 or 128 bars, or the shape stays. Portals: two wormholes, each rests two bars on a wall
  song: scale is every scale the site has (28): the modes, harmonic and melodic minor, the pentatonics, blues, whole
        tone, chromatic, hirajoshi, in sen, iwato, hijaz, bhairav, maqam rast and bayati, slendro, pelog, neutral, and
        the microtonal tunings 19-edo (7 notes or all 19), 24-edo and 31-edo (all their notes: each key is the next step)
  mixer: four faders (all the way down: off). The first 4 white keys mute melody / bass / chords / drums, the next 4
        solo them (their lights show it); the other keys still play. The strip SELECT picks gets the two sends
  fx: SELECT the part (melody, bass, chords, drums) or the returns (the delay and the reverb themselves: delay time,
        repeats, reverb room, brightness; ALGORITHM the delay's tone). Effect: chorus, phaser, flanger, crush, warm,
        fuzz, fold. Filter: left low-pass, right high-pass, the middle open (it stops there on the way through).
        Presets: sound (the sound's own), dry, room, hall, dub delay, slapback, wide, tape, phase, jet, lofi, telephone,
        underwater, crunch
  sound: SELECT "all three" deals a sound set (slime, glass, wood, chip, music box, fm, harp, retro, thumb, dx) and
        shapes all three envelopes together; a part: its own sound and envelope; drums: the drum kit. An FM sound keeps
        its own envelope (its operators are its envelope). FM sounds (SH): tine, bell, harp, bass, written from
        Yamaha's FM 101 recipes and Chowning's bell, not copied from any synth's factory bank
  chords: up to 8 chords that the band loops instead of its own (how many = 0 gives the harmony back to the slime).
        A key sets the chord picked to its note and moves on: play the roots to enter a progression. Types: major,
        min, 7, maj7, min7, dim, aug, sus2, sus4, 6, min6, min7b5, dim7, 9, maj9, min9, 7sus4, add9. PRESETS deals the
        site's 20 famous progressions (ii-V-I, autumn leaves, royal road, lofi loop...) in your key
  drums: your own 16-step pattern, played by the rhythm's drummer (its accents and feel; no fills). The 16 white keys
        are the steps, a black key picks the lane (kick, snare, hat, open hat, rim, conga, tom, ride). The panel shows
        all 8 lanes, the keys' lights the lane picked, the step playing moves along

THE OTHER BUTTONS
  keys        play their note (in the scale) and throw a ball into the tank. What you play steers the band's chord,
              as on the site (the chord of your last notes holds while you play; a progression you entered keeps its own)
  PLAY        plays / stops                                  x2 (a double tap)  from the top
  REC         records a loop of what you play: a count-in ("rec in 4 3 2 1"), you play, REC again and it closes at the
              end of its bar (1, 2, 4 or 8 bars). On a playing loop REC overdubs. The loop is your notes kept in the
              key: it moves with the key and the scale.                                     x2  clears the loop
  SAVE        saves the song (stop first: writing the flash would cut the sound)            x2  loads it back
              hold SAVE + turn PRESETS: song 1, 2 or 3. Stopped for 3 s, the session saves itself and comes back at
              power-on
  ARP         the melody's auto-notes on / off            LFO   the slime on / off (it moves the key, fills, bursts)
  OCT- OCT+   the keyboard's octave (both together: the middle)        MASTER  volume
  knobs: a slow turn moves one step, a fast one sweeps (SLOOP's acceleration)

  notes: none ever sticks to a wall (a note rattling in a corner floats back to the middle; one sunk in a full slime
              melts into it)
  drums: through the site's own drum bus: glue, a little body, the presence dip that keeps them under the melody
  speakers: SLOOP's limiter holds every peak at -5.2 dBFS whatever the knobs do (tested with all of them maxed)

MIDI (the jack in, USB in and out)
  in:  any channel plays like the keys (in the scale, a ball, the loop, the chords); channel 10 plays the drum kit.
       CC 1 / 74 the melody's filter, CC 7 its level, CC 73 / 75 / 72 its attack / decay / release, CC 120 / 123
       stop everything. A MIDI clock sets the tempo; START plays from the top, STOP stops, CONTINUE plays
  out (USB): the melody on channel 1, bass 2, chords 3, drums 10 (GM notes), your keys too, the clock and START /
       STOP (not while a clock comes in). Notes that came in are not sent back out. In the 19- and 31-note scales
       each note goes out with its own pitch bend (+-2 semitones, as GM): the melody round channels 11-16, the
       chords round 4-9, the bass on 2. Set those channels to one sound on the synth that receives them
The LEDs show what is on and which page you are on; a key lights when the melody plays its note; REC blinks for
the count-in and the take, blinks fast while overdubbing, and stays lit while the loop plays.
