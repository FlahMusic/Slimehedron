SLIMEHEDRON FOR THE M-VAVE FM-1 (test build)

This folder: the firmware (slimehedron-fm1.fwsc, always the newest build), INSTALL-ON-FM1.bat, VERSIONS.txt (which
build is which). Built on SLOOP 2.8.5, the first SLOOP that saves and updates on the newer FM-1s (UC25HQ80 flash, sold
from late 2026). Tested in fm1-emu (strict mode, the newer flash). NOT yet run on a real FM-1.

BEFORE YOU INSTALL
- Use a data USB cable (not a charge-only one), plugged straight into the pc (no hub). Chrome or Edge.
- Close other apps that use MIDI. Keep the FM-1 charged; don't let the pc sleep; don't unplug while it writes.
- The official firmware's own sounds cannot be backed up by anyone. Anything made on it stays on it until you go back.

INSTALL
1. Double-click INSTALL-ON-FM1.bat (in this folder). A black window opens and stays open: leave it.
2. Your browser opens http://localhost:8766/webapp/installer/ (if not, open it in Chrome or Edge yourself).
3. The page says SLOOP: that is SLOOP's installer, which is what installs this file. Follow it; let it back up.
4. When it is done, the FM-1 restarts: the slime drops in, gets his headphones and a little synth, his jingle plays,
   then the tank.
   Close the black window.
(A firmware file can't be installed from a plain web page you open from disk: Web MIDI needs a local server, which
 is what the .bat starts. No public installer page takes an arbitrary .fwsc file; each firmware ships its own page.)

IF IT DOES NOT START
- Hold OCT- (alone) while switching the FM-1 on: SLOOP's USB rescue. Then install again from the page.
- Two failed boots in a row also drop into the rescue by themselves.
- Last resort: FM-1-transporter (github.com/kurogedelic/FM-1-transporter), or The Firmware Hub's rescue tool
  (it works on the newer flash; take a full backup first).

BACK TO THE OFFICIAL FIRMWARE
- M-VAVE's updater M-UPGRADE (m-vave.com: Downloads > PC Software > M-UPGRADE) with the official FM-1 firmware V16.
- If M-UPGRADE does not find the FM-1: Clipper's installer page (isod89.github.io/clipper-fm1) > Return to the official
  firmware.

PLAYING IT
At power-on the slime guy drops in, chilling; headphones slide onto his head and a little synth drops into his hands
(surprised, then thrilled), and his jingle plays (any button skips it). Then the
tank waits, standing still: nothing plays until PLAY (it blinks; the screen says "press play"). Set it up first if
you like (a new shape shows at once). Stopped, a key just plays its note. After PLAY the tank moves, the band plays,
and each key also drops a note into the tank ("keys drop notes in", the first time).

Behind the tank: a sunny lake in a forest (Game Boy Color pixels, soft and out of focus), five little pastel slimes
round the edges that hop on each bar's 1, and the site's dancing worm in the bottom right corner: his head always pokes
up and wiggles to the tempo, every 12 bars he rises to dance for 8 (the 4th and 8th in double time), a new colour each
beat. They are decoration only: they never touch the music, and they are drawn only where the screen changes anyway.
They answer you: a key you play makes one little slime hop (low notes on the left, high on the right, so a scale run
walks across them), and when the tank bursts they all jump twice and the worm comes up to dance.

It works like a groovebox: ONE BUTTON, ONE PAGE, every setting in exactly one place.
- The three knobs on the left are the three SOUNDS, on every page: SELECT the melody's, ALGORITHM the bass's,
  PRESETS the chords'. The screen says which sound you landed on.
- The four knobs are the page's. A page with more than four settings has VIEWS: press its button again for the next
  view (the panel's top line says which, e.g. "mixer: pan 2/4"). HOME goes back to the tank.
- Every page but the tank shows its knobs' names and values on a panel under the (smaller) tank.

  shape: triangle, square, pentagon, hexagon, heptagon, octagon, nonagon, decagon, dodecagon, and shards (4..8
        sides, every side its own length). A spinning shape tumbles like a thrown thing. Each wall has HP: it fades as
        notes hit it and breaks open for 2 bars (fewer walls, tougher walls). The solids (cube, octahedron...) and
        the curved, non-Euclidean shapes are next
  sound (ENV): attack, decay, sustain, release for each part; on an FM sound (SMOOTH EP, DX BASS, SH ...) the same
        four reshape its own envelope, 50% = as it was made
  tank: HOME comes back here from any page. A shape or portals set here stay (the slime still bursts and refills the
        tank, it just keeps your shape; untouched, it picks a new one each time). Notes a bar: the melody's density (4, 6, 8, 12, 16; the site aims at 8).
        Portals: two wormholes open at a time, bigger than the site's, each staying 2 bars on one wall, then closing and
        popping open on another; a note that goes in comes out of another hole, on the beat grid. The slime picks portal
        space on about 1 new shape in 6
  song: SCALE (knob 4) runs through 52 scales: the 7 modes; harmonic and melodic minor, harmonic major, phrygian
        dominant (hijaz), double harmonic (bhairav), hungarian minor, lydian dominant, altered, dorian #4, lydian
        augmented, locrian natural 2, neapolitan minor and major, persian, enigmatic, prometheus, bebop dominant; whole
        tone, both diminished, augmented; pentatonic, minor pentatonic, blues, major blues, egyptian, hirajoshi, in sen,
        iwato, kumoi, yo, ryukyu; chromatic; then the ones between the keys: maqam rast, bayati and saba, slendro,
        pelog, 7-edo (thai), just major, the overtone scale, 19-edo (7 notes or all 19), 24-edo, 31-edo (all their
        notes: each key is the next step). The panel spells the whole name out. Keys and MIDI notes always land in it
  mixer: all the way down: off. On any mixer view the first 4 white keys mute melody / bass / chords / drums, the next
        4 solo them (their lights show it); the other keys still play
  fx: effect: chorus, phaser, flanger, crush, warm, fuzz, fold. Filter: left low-pass, right high-pass, the middle
        open (it stops there on the way through). The delay is stereo: its echoes go left, right, left, right
  sound: each sound comes with its own envelope; turning one here makes it yours (a new sound starts from its own).
        An FM sound keeps its own envelope (its operators are its envelope). FM sounds (SH): tine, bell, harp, bass,
        written from Yamaha's FM 101 recipes and Chowning's bell, not copied from any synth's factory bank.
        At power-on the chords are SMOOTH EP (a soft DX7-style electric piano, through the chorus) and the bass BIG
        MONO (an 80s mono-synth pluck). Also new: DX BASS (the 80s FM bass), POLY BRASS and DREAM STAB (80s synth
        chords). The DX ones are shaped by measurement to sound like the famous DX7 E.PIANO 1 (made softer) and BASS 1,
        with our own settings: no factory data
  slime: with the slime on (LFO), the melody and the chords get softer and darker as the goo fills the tank you see,
        and the melody a little roomier; a burst opens them again as the goo drains. Gentle: about half the site's
  chords: up to 8 chords that the band loops instead of its own (how many = 0 gives the harmony back to the slime).
        A key sets the chord picked to its note and moves on: play the roots to enter a progression. Types: major,
        min, 7, maj7, min7, dim, aug, sus2, sus4, 6, min6, min7b5, dim7, 9, maj9, min9, 7sus4, add9. Famous ones: the
        site's 20 famous progressions (ii-V-I, autumn leaves, royal road, lofi loop...) in your key
  drums: the site's own drums, made on the FM-1 the way the site makes them (the kick's pitch dive and beater click,
        the snare's noise and shell, the 808 metal hats, the open hat the next hat cuts off, the ride tuned to the key,
        toms, congas, clave). PATTERN: your own 16 steps, played by the rhythm's drummer (no fills): the 16 white keys
        are the steps, a black key picks the lane; turning to "mine" with nothing in it starts from the rhythm's bar.
        KIT: any rhythm's kit for every rhythm ("rhythm": each plays its own), tuned down 2 or up 12 semitones, its
        decay from half to twice as long; SWING: how late every other 16th lands (0: straight, 50%: nearly a triplet).
        With the slime on it breathes between 13% and 31% by itself until you turn it (the site's swing does the same)
  midi: see MIDI below

THE OTHER BUTTONS
  keys        play their note (in the scale) and throw a ball into the tank. What you play steers the band's chord,
              as on the site (the chord of your last notes holds while you play; a progression you entered keeps its own)
  PLAY        plays / stops                                  x2 (a double tap)  from the top
  REC         nothing for now (its loop went: it came back at power-on with no way to delete it). It will become
              projects: save, load and delete everything you set
  SAVE        the MIDI page. Saving needs nothing: stopped for 3 s, everything you set saves itself to the FM-1 and
              comes back at power-on ("welcome back")
  ARP         the band's chords as an arpeggio, on / off, and its page (pattern, rate, octaves, gate)
              LFO   the slime on / off (it moves the key, the swing breathes, the goo muffles, it bursts)
  OCT- OCT+   the keyboard's octave (both together: the middle)        MASTER  volume
  knobs: a slow turn moves one step, a fast one sweeps (SLOOP's acceleration)

  notes: none ever sticks to a wall (a note rattling in a corner floats back to the middle; one sunk in a full slime
              melts into it). The balls bend their speed a little so their bounces land on the beat grid, and the
              screen shows the tank as you HEAR it: a bounce is drawn when its note sounds
  drums: through the site's own drum bus: glue, a little body, the presence dip that keeps them under the melody
  speakers: SLOOP's limiter holds every peak at -5.2 dBFS whatever the knobs do (tested with all of them maxed)

MIDI (the jack in, USB in and out; the MIDI page is SAVE)
  in:  every channel (or the one "in channel" picks) plays like the keys: in the scale, a ball, the loop, the chords.
       The drums' channel (10 unless you change it) plays the drum kit. CC 1 / 74 the melody's filter, CC 7 its level,
       CC 73 / 75 / 72 its attack / decay / release, CC 120 / 123 stop everything. With "sync: follow", a MIDI
       clock sets the tempo and START plays from the top, STOP stops, CONTINUE plays ("own": it keeps its own)
  out (USB): each part on its own channel (melody 1, bass 2, chords 3, drums 10 unless you change them; "off" sends
       nothing for that part), your keys too; with "clock: out" the clock and START / STOP (not while a clock comes
       in). Notes that came in are not sent back out; "notes: off" sends no notes at all. In a scale with notes
       between the keys each note goes out with its own pitch bend (+-2 semitones, as GM) on its part's channel, just
       before it (the FM-1 has no MPE: two chord notes bent differently at once share the later bend)
The LEDs show what is on and which page you are on; a key lights when the melody plays its note.
