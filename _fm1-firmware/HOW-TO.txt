SLIMEHEDRON FOR THE M-VAVE FM-1 (test build)

This folder: the firmware (slimehedron-fm1.fwsc, always the newest build), INSTALL-ON-FM1.bat, VERSIONS.txt (which
build is which). Built on SLOOP 2.8.5, the first SLOOP that saves and updates on the newer FM-1s (UC25HQ80 flash, sold
from late 2026). Tested in fm1-emu (strict mode, the newer flash). NOT yet run on a real FM-1.

BEFORE YOU INSTALL
- Use a data USB cable (not a charge-only one), plugged straight into the pc (no hub). Chrome or Edge.
- Close other apps that use MIDI. Keep the FM-1 charged; don't let the pc sleep; don't unplug while it writes.
- The official firmware's own sounds cannot be backed up by anyone. Anything made on it stays on it until you go back.

INSTALL
1. Double-click INSTALL-ON-FM1.bat (in this folder). A black window opens and stays open: leave it.
2. Your browser opens http://localhost:8766/webapp/installer/ (if not, open it in Chrome or Edge yourself).
3. The page says SLOOP: that is SLOOP's installer, which is what installs this file. Follow it; let it back up.
4. When it is done, the FM-1 restarts and shows "slimehedron", then the tank. Close the black window.
(A firmware file can't be installed from a plain web page you open from disk: Web MIDI needs a local server, which
 is what the .bat starts. No public installer page takes an arbitrary .fwsc file; each firmware ships its own page.)

IF IT DOES NOT START
- Hold OCT- (alone) while switching the FM-1 on: SLOOP's USB rescue. Then install again from the page.
- Two failed boots in a row also drop into the rescue by themselves.
- Last resort: FM-1-transporter (github.com/kurogedelic/FM-1-transporter), or The Firmware Hub's rescue tool
  (it works on the newer flash; take a full backup first).

BACK TO THE OFFICIAL FIRMWARE
- M-VAVE's updater M-UPGRADE (m-vave.com: Downloads > PC Software > M-UPGRADE) with the official FM-1 firmware V16.
- If M-UPGRADE does not find the FM-1: Clipper's installer page (isod89.github.io/clipper-fm1) > Return to the official
  firmware.

PLAYING IT
Five pages. The top line says where you are and what you just changed; the bottom line names the four knobs.

BUTTONS (the same on every page; "x2" = a double tap, two presses quickly)
  keys        play their note now (in the scale) and throw a ball into the tank
  PLAY        everything runs / stands still          x2  from the top (bar 1)
  HOME        back to the home page; on the home page: a new shape (on the next downbeat)
  FX          the fx page: press again for the next part, melody > bass > chords > drums > home
  ENV         the notes page (attack, decay...): press again for the next part, melody > bass > chords > home
  EDIT        the sound page            GLO     the mix page        (press again: home)
  ARP         auto-notes on / off
  LFO         the slime on / off (key journey, the tank fills, bursts, reforms)     x2  pin the key (it stops moving)
  SEQ         the band on / off (drums, bass and chords)                            x2  the drums alone / the band back
  SCL         next scale                                                            x2  the scale before
  OCT- / OCT+ the keyboard's octave (both together: back to the middle)
  REC         record a loop: it counts you in (clicks, "rec in 4 3 2 1"), you play, REC again and the loop closes at
              the end of its bar (1, 2, 4 or 8 bars). On a playing loop, REC = overdub (play more into it), REC again
              to stop. The loop is your notes, kept in the key: it moves with the key and the scale. x2  clear the loop
  SAVE        save the song (stop first: writing the flash would cut the sound)     x2  load the song back
              hold SAVE + turn PRESETS: pick the song, 1 to 3
              The session also keeps itself: stopped for 3 s, it is written, and it comes back when you switch on
  MASTER      volume

KNOBS                    KNOB 1     KNOB 2           KNOB 3     KNOB 4     SELECT        ALGORITHM     PRESETS
  HOME                   tempo      spin             rhythm     key        walls 3..12   space         melody sound
  FX (one part)          filter     drive (drums:    echo       space      character     amount        fx preset
                                    punch)
  NOTES (ENV, one part)  attack     decay            sustain    release    which part    glide         note shape
  SOUND (EDIT)           melody     bass             chords     drum kit   note length   chord length  sound set
  MIX (GLO)              melody     bass             chords     drums      swing         echo time     room size

  filter: left is low-pass, right is high-pass, the middle is open (it stops there on the way through)
  character: chorus, phaser, flanger, crush, warm, fuzz, fold; amount turns its depth and its mix together
  fx presets: sound (the sound's own), dry, room, hall, dub echo, slapback, wide, tape, phase, jet, lofi, telephone,
              underwater, crunch
  note shapes: the sound's own, pluck, keys, soft, pad, stab (a new sound brings its own back; an FM sound always
              keeps its own: its operators are its envelope)
  sound sets: slime, glass, wood, chip, music box, fm, harp, retro, thumb, dx (the 6-operator FM sounds below)
  FM sounds (SH, on the 6-operator engine): tine (electric piano, melody or chords), bell, harp, FM bass. Written
              from Yamaha's own FM 101 recipes and Chowning's bell, not copied from any synth's factory bank
  knobs: a slow turn moves one step (fine tuning), a fast turn sweeps (SLOOP's acceleration)
  drums: through the site's own drum bus: a light glue compressor, a little body, and the presence dip that keeps
              them under the melody where ears are most sensitive (each kit's glue measured against the site's)
  speakers: SLOOP's limiter holds every peak at -5.2 dBFS whatever the knobs do (tested with all of them maxed)
  rhythms: pop rock disco bossa jazz samba conga march chip beep industrial, each with its own fills and feel
  scales: major minor pentatonic minor-pentatonic dorian mixolydian 19-note 31-note
  mix: each part from its measured level, down to off
The LEDs show what is on and which page you are on; a key lights when the melody plays its note; REC blinks for
the count-in and the take, blinks fast while overdubbing, and stays lit while the loop plays.
