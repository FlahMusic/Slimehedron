# Slimehedron — Learn Mode Curriculum Blueprint
_For your approval before I build any lesson code. Free forever · no streaks · no guilt · no nags._

## The philosophy (what I'm building toward)
A real learning ground, not a tacked-on demo — and the opposite of the Duolingo trap. Grounded in what actually works for teaching kids music (the proven methods all share this spine):

1. **Steady beat → rhythm → pitch → harmony → emotion → creation.** This order is near-universal among great teachers. We already start with rhythm — correct.
2. **Prepare → Present → Practice.** Kids *experience* a concept through play FIRST, then it gets named, then they practice it. Never name-then-drill.
3. **Ear first, symbol second.** Hear it and do it before seeing any notation (how you learn a language).
4. **Patterns, not single notes.** Teach little melodic/rhythmic shapes, not isolated facts.
5. **Emotion is age-staged (research-backed):** under ~5 hear happy/sad mostly through *tempo & energy*; the "major = happy / minor = sad" wiring locks in around **age 6–8**. So the emotion path leads with tempo/energy for littles, then layers mode for older kids — same lesson, deeper on demand.
6. **Do, don't watch.** Every lesson ends with the child MAKING sound (play by touch, computer keys, or MIDI), not passively tapping "next."

## What exists today (the foundation — we build ON this, not over it)
- **Rhythm** (steady beat first — good instinct, keep + expand)

Honest assessment: the bones are right, but it's thin and jumps too fast from "find keys" to "seven modes." It needs more small rungs between, more *play-and-create* per concept, and an emotion/expression thread.

## Proposed course map (each course = an island; lessons build in small rungs)

### Island 1 — PULSE (rhythm & time) — _the ground floor, littlest kids_
1. Feel the beat (tap along to a groove — the slime bounces on the beat)
2. Fast & slow (tempo = energy; run vs. tiptoe)
3. Long & short (a steady beat vs. notes that fill it — no notation yet, just sound)
4. Loud & soft (dynamics = feeling)
5. Simple patterns (echo a 2-beat pattern back — call & response)
6. **Make a beat** (build your own 4-beat loop on the pads → it plays in the band)

### Island 2 — PITCH (high/low, the keyboard, scales)
1. High & low (which slime sings higher?)
2. Find the keys (exists — keep)
3. Steps & skips (moving by neighbors vs. jumps — the singable idea)
4. The major scale as a *feeling of home* (do-re-mi… → landing on "home" feels finished)
5. Minor scale (same steps, one note lower = a different color)
6. Pentatonic (the "no wrong notes" playground — improvise freely)
7. **Sing/play a little tune** (guided 5-note pattern → then free)

### Island 3 — COLOR & FEELING (modes & emotion) — _leads with energy, deepens to mode_
1. Happy or sad? (tempo + energy first — works for ages 3+)
2. The one note that changes everything (major 3rd vs minor 3rd — the emotional hinge)
3. Meet the modes as *moods* (bright Lydian = dreamy, dark Phrygian = mysterious…) — the Midjourney modal scenes finally get used here as emotional worlds
4. Match the mood (hear a snippet → pick the feeling)
5. **Paint with a mode** (pick a mode, the auto-band plays it, you improvise the melody)

### Island 4 — HARMONY (chords & how notes stack)
1. Two notes together (consonant vs. spicy — intervals, exists, fold in)
2. Build a triad (exists — keep, PLAY-to-advance)
3. Major vs minor triad (the feeling hinge again, now in chords)
4. Inversions / 7ths / color chords (exists — keep as the "deep end")
5. **A chord under a melody** (play a chord, hear how it supports the tune)

### Island 5 — CREATE (put it together) — _the payoff_
1. Melody + beat (your tune over your loop)
2. Melody + chords (harmony under it)
3. Full band jam (auto-band + you = the whole thing)
4. **Record & keep** it (already have recording — surface it here as the graduation)

## How each lesson builds on the last (Rocksmith-style scaffolding, no boredom)
- Every new lesson **reuses something learned** (e.g. "steps & skips" reappears inside "build a tune" and again in "inversions").
- **Tiny rungs, quick wins** — a lesson is 60–120 seconds of doing, then you're making music, not drilling flashcards.
- **Always ends in creation/play**, so it never feels like homework.
- Difficulty **adapts gently**: if a kid nails it fast, offer the "go deeper" variant; if not, no penalty, no red X, just try again.
