Slimehedron for teachers

A free music-theory toy for children, in a browser tab.

Open Slimehedron

What you need to know first

  • No signup. No accounts, for you or the children.
  • No data leaves the device. Nothing is collected, nothing is sent anywhere. Lesson progress is kept in the browser on that device and nowhere else.
  • No ads, no purchases, no subscription. There is nothing to buy.
  • Nothing to install. It is one web page. It works offline once it has loaded.
  • Free and open source under the GPL-3.0. The code is on GitHub.

In practice that means you can open it on a locked-down school network without a purchase order, a data-processing agreement or a privacy review.

Put one link on the board

Add ?l= and a lesson name to the address and every child lands on that exact lesson, with no menu to get lost in.

?m=play, ?m=studio and ?m=learn open a whole mode the same way.

The three rooms

  • Learn — 19 numbered lessons, in order. Nothing is locked; the numbers show the path.
  • Play — a sandbox. Sound happens in the first second, no reading required.
  • Studio — the full thing: scales, chords, a step sequencer, MIDI.
  • Tools — metronome, circle of fifths, chord builder and drum machine, each on its own screen.

The lessons, and their links

Games: ?l=echo (call and response), ?l=updown (melodic direction), ?l=findhome (find the tonic). Review: ?l=review.

From lesson 11 the key moves between questions. The lesson plays the tonic first, then the note to find, so the child is comparing two sounds rather than remembering a position on the screen. Lessons 1–10 stay in one key on purpose.

Colour is information. Every scale degree has one colour, in every lesson and every key — the tonic is always the same colour, the third always another. A lowered degree keeps its natural’s colour, because it is the same degree.

Why the lessons are in that order

The sequence follows the Kodály order as published by the Holy Names University Kodály Center, cross-checked against the Trinity College London theory syllabus and the DfE Model Music Curriculum.

Two parts of it surprise most adults:

  • The step after the major pentatonic is not the major scale. It is the la-pentatonic — the same five notes with a different one as home. Kodály puts it two grades before the major scale.
  • fa arrives a whole grade before ti, and the major scale is built up rather than handed over whole.

Modes sit at the very end, and Aeolian is introduced as a second name for the natural minor — which is how Trinity frames it. Phrygian, Lydian and Locrian are not included: they do not appear in any primary-level syllabus we could find.

Things worth knowing in a classroom

  • There is no fail state. A wrong answer repeats the question. Nothing is lost, there is no timer, no streak and no score to protect.
  • Instructions are read aloud, so a child who cannot read yet can still start a lesson alone. There is a speaker button on every lesson to turn it off.
  • Headphones or a speaker are needed. It is all sound.
  • Colour-blind mode is a checkbox in the bottom-left corner of the front page. It repaints everything, not just the menus.
  • Progress is per-device and per-browser. On shared machines, or if the browser clears its storage, it starts again. There is no login to fix this, by design.
  • A MIDI keyboard works if you have one — plug it in, it is picked up automatically.
